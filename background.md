## Background
This decision pertains to the career trajectory of young people who would consider pursuing a red seal in favour of entering the white collar workforce upon graduating with their business degree, specifically an information systems or adjacent degree. 

The decision focuses on opportunity costs of alternative career paths, primarily given economic conditions, comparing income and cost of living in provincial hubs for those jobs, as well as how pursuit of further education impacts wealth over time.

Target jobs for the direct entry option include business analyst, information/data engineer, SOC analyst, or adjacent jobs. These are direct tech work experience in the field studied. 

An I&C Technician diploma would postpone entry into the workforce, thus postponing income, but would develop skills and qualifications that open an alternative career path. A red seal trade would provide redundancy in case the white collar marketplace faces turbulence (Canada, 2020), as some fear due to craze in AI, while also developing unique and valuable skills in Industrial Control Systems ICS and Programmable Logical Controllers PLCs, which would stand out in IT, data or cybersecurity roles in operational technology sectors in future. 

Search results for “got a degree, should I do trades?” and adjacent searches are focused on the university vs. trade-school tradeoff at the high school graduate level. There is limited focus on considering one post accomplishing the other beyond reddit and quora threads. 

When making career decisions, (CFNC, 2022) recommend assessment of your situation using 10 *things, which can be summarized as your personality / interests, your goals, your options given your current skills & position, and the opportunities associated with alternative routes. A constraint on our model is the 3rd; we can simulate all we want, but getting that outcome in real life relies on proper execution. 

(Bowen, 2026) outline multiple models for forecasting income. We are simulating alternative career routes, but considering for lateral & vertical career movements. Thus our forecast will be a sum of multiple straight line calculations. 

This decision will impact short term financial position; earnings over the 3 years of I&C tech education would be less than that of full time, while having associated costs. And long term position; via differentials in wages between careers, and differentials in cost of living differential between employment locations. 

However, costs in Alberta / Saskatchewan, the largest employers in I&C are lower than the Canadian average, positioning for a potential lower cost career, allowing for higher allocations to savings and investment.
